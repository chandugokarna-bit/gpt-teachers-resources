Activity 2: Critical Group Discussion Questions (Click Box Activity)

Click on the key historical questions below to open and analyze their official answer boxes:

1. How did British land revenue policies force Indian farmers into deep debt?
The British strictly collected extremely high revenue strictly in the form of cash. No exemptions were granted even during severe famines. To pay taxes on time, farmers had to borrow money at high interest rates from moneylenders, eventually losing their lands and falling into a vicious debt trap.
2. What caused the collapse of traditional weaving and cottage industries in India?
Due to the Industrial Revolution in England, cheap machine-made clothes produced in its mills flooded the Indian market. Since the British imposed heavy duties on Indian handloom textiles, local artisans could not compete, leading to the destruction of cottage industries.
3. What were the main recommendations and goals of the Macaulay Report?
Macaulay recommended the implementation of a modern English-medium education system in India. The goal was to create a class of clerical workers to assist British administration, creating individuals who were Indian in blood and color, but British in tastes, opinions, morals, and intellect.
4. Why did the British expand railway and telegraph facilities across India?
They developed railways to swiftly transport raw materials from rural India to ports, distribute British finished goods to remote corners of the country, and mobilize British military forces rapidly to suppress local rebellions.
5. What new social classes emerged in India due to British administration?
The British system generated an influential 'modern middle class' comprising English-educated lawyers, doctors, teachers, journalists, and government employees, alongside a vast class of marginalized, landless agricultural laborers.
6. What does Dadabhai Naoroji's 'Drain of Wealth Theory' explain?
This theory explains how India's immense wealth was systematically siphoned off to England in the form of land revenue, salaries, pensions, and trade profits without getting any proportional returns, reducing India to a completely impoverished nation.
