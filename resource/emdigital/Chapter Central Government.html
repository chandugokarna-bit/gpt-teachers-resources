Activity 2: Click Box Panel on Parliamentary Democracy

Click on any of the group discussion queries below to unlock their official legislative resolution status live:

1. Why did India choose to adopt a Parliamentary form of government?
India was under British rule for a long duration, making the parliamentary framework familiar to the public. In this system, the Executive is directly accountable to the Legislature (Parliament), leaving no room for authoritarian dictatorship.
2. What are the minimum essential criteria required to qualify as a member of the Lok Sabha?
To qualify as a member of the Lok Sabha, an individual: 1) Must be a citizen of India. 2) Must have completed at least 25 years of age. 3) Must not hold any office of profit under the government, and must not be insolvent or unsound of mind.
3. Why is the Rajya Sabha referred to as a 'Permanent House'?
Unlike the Lok Sabha, the Rajya Sabha is never dissolved all at once. The term of office for its members is 6 years, with one-third (1/3) of the members retiring every two years, who are subsequently replaced by newly elected members.
4. What are the primary powers and executive functions of the Prime Minister of India?
The Prime Minister selects ministers and recommends them to the President, allocates administrative portfolios among them, and presides over Union Cabinet meetings to direct the country's comprehensive policy decisions.
5. What are the constitutional 'Emergency Powers' vested in the President of India?
The President has the authority to declare three kinds of emergencies: 1) National Emergency (Article 352). 2) State Emergency or President's Rule (Article 356). 3) Financial Emergency (Article 360).
6. What is meant by the 'Collective Responsibility' of the Union Council of Ministers?
The Council of Ministers is jointly accountable to the Lok Sabha for all its administrative decisions and actions. If a 'No-Confidence Motion' is successfully passed against the government in the Lok Sabha, the entire Council of Ministers must resign.
