Activity 2: Descriptive Accordion of the Parliamentary System

Click on the crucial conceptual questions below to unlock their official answer boxes live:

1. What are the three organs of government and what are their functions?
The three organs of government are: 1) Legislature - It frames the laws required for the country. 2) Executive - It implements the formulated laws. 3) Judiciary - It punishes lawbreakers and protects justice.
2. What is the legislature called in India and what are its divisions?
The central legislature of India is called 'Parliament'. It consists of two houses: the Upper House (Rajya Sabha) and the Lower House (Lok Sabha).
3. What is the difference between 'Nominal' and 'Real' executives in the Executive branch?
The President of India is the constitutional 'Nominal' head of the country. However, the Cabinet headed by the Prime Minister is the 'Real' executive that runs the entire administration of the country.
4. What is meant by the 'Independent and Impartial' policy of the Judiciary?
The Judiciary is a system that delivers justice impartially based solely on evidence and bound by the Constitution, without any political pressure or interference from the Legislature and the Executive.
5. How does a 'Bill' become an 'Act' in Parliament?
First, the draft copy of any law (Bill) must be discussed and passed by a majority in both the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha. Later, when the President signs it, it officially becomes an 'Act' (Law).
6. How can it be said that India has an 'Integrated Judicial System'?
Because the entire judiciary in India, from rural courts to the country's highest Supreme Court, is linked as a single chain. The judgment delivered by the Supreme Court applies to all courts across the nation.
